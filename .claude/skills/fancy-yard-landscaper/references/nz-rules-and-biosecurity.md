# NZ Rules, Biosecurity and Safety Checks

This is a checklist of what to verify. It is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules differ by council and change over time. Tell the user to confirm with their council, and cite the source to check each time.

## 1. Council and district plan

Check with the local council (city or district):

- Maximum fence and hedge height on boundaries and near driveways
- Recession planes and shading rules where they apply to structures
- Building consent for: retaining walls (height and load limits apply), decks, pergolas, taller fences, pool barriers, sheds
- Resource consent triggers: heritage sites, coastal areas, flood zones, significant slope, overlays
- Protected or notable trees: many councils keep a register. Check before pruning or removing a large tree.
- Stormwater: how much of the section must stay permeable; where roof runoff must go

Where to look: council website (district plan and property search), or phone the council duty planner.

## 2. Boundary and neighbours

- **Fencing Act 1978**: covers shared boundary fences and cost-sharing between neighbours. Neighbours usually talk first; a notice process exists if they cannot agree. Confirm current details at the Community Law or Government websites.
- **Property Law Act 2007**: has provisions for trees and hedges that block sunlight or views, or cause damage. Confirm current details before advising.
- Talk to neighbours before planting a tall hedge or large tree on or near the boundary.
- Plant so you can clip the hedge from your own side (leave 0.5-1 m).

## 3. Services and power lines

- **Before you dig**: request plans from **beforeudig.co.nz** for underground services (water, gas, power, telecommunications).
- **Overhead lines**: keep trees well clear. The Electricity (Hazards from Trees) Regulations 2003 apply. Contact the line owner before planting near lines or pruning trees that touch them. Do not prune trees near power lines yourself.
- Keep plants and roots away from sewer and stormwater lines, septic tanks and drain fields.
- Keep sightlines clear at driveways and corners.

## 4. Biosecurity

- **National Pest Plant Accord (NPPA)**: a list of plants that cannot be sold, propagated or distributed in NZ. Check the current list before recommending a species. Examples often cited include Chinese privet, tree privet and Japanese honeysuckle; verify against the current list.
- **Regional pest management plans**: regional councils list plants that must be controlled in their area. Check the local regional council.
- **Myrtle rust**: affects Myrtaceae (pōhutukawa, rātā, mānuka, kānuka, ramarama, feijoa, lilly pilly). Buy from reputable nurseries. Report suspected infection to MPI (Biosecurity NZ, 0800 80 99 66).
- **Kauri dieback**: in kauri areas, clean footwear, tools and vehicles, and do not move soil or plant material out of infected areas.
- Do not dump garden waste in bush, reserves or roadside. Weeds spread from dumped waste.
- Avoid known environmental weeds even if they are sold at garden centres. Ask the regional council for current lists.

## 5. Safety

- **Toxic plants**: check before planting near children, pets or stock. Examples include karaka kernels, tutu, oleander, angel's trumpet (Brugmansia), foxglove. For suspected poisoning in people, call the National Poisons Centre (0800 764 766) or 111 in an emergency. For animals, call a vet.
- **Sun**: UV levels are high in NZ summer. Plan shade in outdoor living areas.
- **Trees**: use a qualified arborist for large trees, work near buildings or work near power lines.
- **Ladders and chainsaws**: use protective equipment and do not work alone at height.
- **Fire risk** (dry regions): keep flammable shrubs and dead material away from buildings; check Fire and Emergency NZ guidance (checkitsalright.nz).

## 6. Water

- Many councils apply summer water restrictions. Check before planning irrigation or lawn.
- Use mulch, rain tanks (check council rules), and drought-resilient plants in dry regions.
- Water young plants deeply and less often to encourage deep roots.

## 7. What to verify with the user

Ask before finalising a plan:

1. Which region and suburb?
2. Coastal or sheltered? Windy?
3. Frost: none, light, or hard?
4. Soil: clay, sandy, pumice, rocky, wet?
5. Which direction does the main outdoor space face?
6. Boundary: shared fence, height wanted, neighbours' views?
7. Pets, children, stock, or wildlife issues?
8. Time and budget (NZ$) each year for upkeep?
9. Any council overlay, covenant or body corporate rules?

If a fact cannot be confirmed from the user's answers, say so, state the assumption, and name who to ask (council, nursery, arborist, MPI).
