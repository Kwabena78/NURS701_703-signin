# Chartwell front yard: three sample designs

Pick one (or mix) tomorrow. Sketches are in `design-options.html`. Same site, same fixed items in all three: steel edging on all lines, basalt chip in the path strip (Zone C), utility box screened, bins screened, low-voltage lighting, no Myrtaceae (myrtle rust) and no Buxus (blight).

Sizes, growth and prices are not stated as fact. Confirm with a Hamilton nursery. Quantities need your measurements.

## Option 1: Layered Edge (recommended, the plan we have)

**Idea:** One strong specimen tree, blocks of grasses, clipped hedge as a backdrop.

- Tree: multi-stem kōwhai (*Sophora* 'Dragon's Gold' or *S. microphylla*), off-centre in bed A
- Bed A: *Lomandra* or *Carex testacea* mass, *Astelia* 'Silver Spear' x3, *Corokia* 'Geenty's Green' around utility box
- Bed D: clipped hedge (existing if not lilly pilly, else *Griselinia* 'Broadway Mint') with *Liriope* or *Dianella* ribbon
- Door: two black pots with Corokia or Pittosporum spheres

**Best for:** Street presence with native identity and birds (tūī).
**Maintenance:** Medium. Clip hedge 2 to 3 times a year, water first two summers.
**Watch:** Kōwhai drops leaves briefly in spring and needs drainage. Plant in autumn.

## Option 2: Native Modern

**Idea:** All native, textural and layered, no clipped hedge. Looks natural against the black-and-white house.

- Tree: tī kōuka (cabbage tree, *Cordyline australis*) or kōwhai
- Bed A: *Libertia peregrinans* (orange), *Carex testacea*, *Astelia* 'Silver Spear', *Coprosma* 'Taiko' or similar low Coprosma
- Bed D: mixed native screen: *Pittosporum tenuifolium* cultivar and *Griselinia* in alternating blocks, 2 to 3 species
- Ground: *Muehlenbeckia axillaris* and *Dichondra* creeping through gravel
- Door: harakeke (flax) cultivar in a tall pot, or *Astelia* in pots

**Best for:** Lowest inputs, best for birds and insects, strongest "local" feel.
**Maintenance:** Low to medium. Light shaping, no formal clipping.
**Watch:** Looks untidy if plants are not spaced and edged. Some tussocks self-seed; check the Waikato regional pest plan for anything you choose.

## Option 3: Formal Clean

**Idea:** Symmetry and evergreen structure. Crisp and low effort, suited to the geometric house.

- Trees: pair of *Magnolia* 'Little Gem' (or one, off-centre) in raised charcoal planters or beds
- Hedge: continuous clipped *Griselinia* or *Corokia* along the fence lines, all at one height
- Ground: *Liriope* 'Evergreen Giant' blocks, basalt chip, lawn reduced to a strict rectangle
- Door: matching black pots with clipped Pittosporum 'Golf Ball' or Corokia spheres

**Best for:** Minimum surprises, neat all year, easy to sell.
**Maintenance:** Medium (regular clipping) but simple.
**Watch:** Least seasonal change and lowest wildlife value. Magnolia needs steady water.

## Quick comparison

| | Option 1 Layered Edge | Option 2 Native Modern | Option 3 Formal Clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wow factor | High (specimen tree) | High (texture) | Medium (crisp) |
| Birds and wildlife | High | Highest | Low |
| Weekly effort | Medium | Low | Medium |
| Year-round look | Good | Good | Best |
| Sensitive to poor drainage | Kōwhai, Astelia | Astelia | Magnolia |
| Relative cost (planting) | Medium | Medium | Medium to high (planters, more hedge) |
