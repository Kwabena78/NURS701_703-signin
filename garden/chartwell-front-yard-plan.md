# Front Yard Design Plan: 39 [street], Chartwell, Hamilton

Drafted 30 September 2026 from five photos. Planning model: Fable 5.1. Implementation to follow on a cheaper model.

## 1. What I can see

**House and setting**
- Two-storey modern build: white horizontal weatherboard, charcoal panels and joinery, black vertical-batten fencing, exposed-aggregate driveway with a channel drain, plain concrete path to the front door.
- New subdivision streetscape. Neighbours both sides are similar builds with the same fence style. Kerb-and-channel, no street tree in front of your section yet.
- Photos are spring (early leaf on the deciduous tree), overcast, no visible frost damage.

**Existing front garden**
- Small rectangular lawn, fresh turf, healthy.
- Two mulched beds with bark chip: one left of the path (about 3 × 3 m) and one narrow strip on the right boundary (about 1 × 6 m). Both hold young glossy-leaved hedge shrubs, roughly 0.6–0.9 m tall, planted about 0.8 m apart. They look like **lilly pilly (Syzygium)** or possibly Portuguese laurel. This matters: lilly pilly is Myrtaceae and is a myrtle rust host. **Identify before deciding to keep them** (see Section 6).
- One young deciduous tree, single stem, about 2.5 m, in the left bed. Unidentified from the photo.
- A small young tree (looks like a fruit tree or crab apple) at the far right by the neighbour's garage.
- Clumping grasses (look like *Lomandra*) in the strip between the path and the driveway.
- A grass strip about 0.5 m wide between the concrete path and the driveway.
- Utility box (green, likely fibre or gas) plus a white marker post in the left front corner of the lawn.
- Black letterbox post (#39) on the driveway side.
- Wheelie bins stored by the left side gate, visible from the street.
- Side gates are black batten, matching the fences.

**Strengths to build on**
- Clean, consistent colour palette (white, charcoal, black). Any planting will read strongly against it.
- Existing hedge lines already frame the space.
- Good bones: drainage in place, turf healthy, no obvious wet patches.

**Weak points**
- The garden currently looks like every other section on the street: lawn, bark, hedge plants in a row.
- No focal point. The single deciduous tree is thin and off-centre.
- Bark chip is scattering onto the path and driveway.
- The grass strip between the path and drive is hard to mow and will look ragged.
- The utility box and bins are the first things a visitor sees.
- The narrow right-hand bed is one straight row of one species.
- No lighting, no seasonal change, no height variation.

## 2. Assumptions (correct me)

1. **Aspect unknown.** I have assumed the front faces roughly north or east (the photo light is flat, so I cannot tell). If it faces south, the plant palette needs shade-tolerant swaps. See the questions at the end.
2. **Frontage about 14 m wide, front garden about 5–6 m deep** from kerb to house. Estimated from the photos. Please measure.
3. **Soil**: Hamilton ash over clay, compacted by construction, probably with builder's rubble under the turf. Assume poor drainage in the beds until proven otherwise.
4. **Climate (Hamilton)**: humid, light to moderate winter frosts, hot and often dry January to March, wet winters. Last frost usually by late September or early October. Indicative; confirm with a local nursery.
5. **Covenant**: most new Hamilton subdivisions carry design covenants (front fence height, minimum landscaping, no visible bins, sometimes an approved plant list). I assume one exists.
6. **Time budget**: about 1–2 hours a week in the growing season, less in winter.

## 3. Design concept: "Layered edge"

The aim for a top-tier street presence is not more plants. It is structure, contrast and one strong idea. For this house:

- **Keep the geometry.** The house is rectilinear. The garden should be too: straight steel edges, blocks of a single plant, one specimen tree as the only "loose" element.
- **Three heights.** Low groundcover and grass masses (0.2–0.4 m), mid-height clipped or mounded shrubs (0.8–1.2 m), one multi-stem specimen tree (4–6 m mature).
- **Native plus exotic.** Natives for birds and tolerance of Hamilton soils; a few exotics for winter structure and colour.
- **Black, silver and lime.** Foliage colour that sits with the house: silver-grey (*Astelia*), lime (*Lomandra*, *Coprosma*), deep green (hedge), one seasonal colour hit from the tree.
- **Night presence.** Three or four low-voltage lights turn a good garden into a memorable one from the street.
- **Hide the ugly.** Utility box, bins, and the ragged grass strip.

## 4. Zone-by-zone plan

### Zone A: Left front bed (the hero bed, about 3 × 3 m, expand to about 3.5 × 4 m)

1. **Specimen tree.** Replace or relocate the thin deciduous tree with a **multi-stem kōwhai** (*Sophora* 'Dragon's Gold' for small spaces, or *Sophora microphylla* if you have room) for tūī in spring and native credibility, **or** an evergreen **Magnolia 'Little Gem'** for formal year-round structure. Kōwhai is my recommendation: it is the one plant that will make the street stop. Position it about 1.5 m in from the path, not centred, so it is seen from the front door and the street.
2. **Understorey mass.** Under and around the tree: a block of 9–12 **Lomandra 'Tanika'** or **Carex testacea** (orange tussock; native, loves Hamilton). Plant on a 0.5 m grid.
3. **Silver accent.** Three **Astelia 'Silver Spear'** near the front corner, spaced 0.8 m.
4. **Utility box.** Plant a low **Corokia 'Geenty's Green'** hedge (clip to 0.6 m) in an L around the box on the street and lawn sides, leaving the access face open. Keep 0.5 m clear of the box door. Confirm with the utility owner which face must stay clear.
5. **Edge.** Replace the plastic garden edge with **black powder-coated steel** or corten edging, 100 mm high, straight lines.

### Zone B: Front lawn

- Keep it. A sharp rectangle of lawn is the frame that makes the beds read. Reduce it slightly to enlarge Zone A.
- Steel-edge every lawn side. This is the single biggest visual upgrade for the least money.
- Feed and overseed in autumn. Mow at 40–50 mm in summer to survive Hamilton dry spells.

### Zone C: Path and driveway strip

- Remove the 0.5 m grass strip between the path and driveway. Fill with the same clumping grass as the existing *Lomandra* strip so the path reads as one wide band. Alternatively, lay 400 × 400 mm charcoal concrete pavers with 50 mm gaps planted with *Muehlenbeckia axillaris* or *Dichondra*. This removes a mowing problem and widens the entry.
- Replace bark in this strip with **black or dark-grey basalt chip (10–14 mm)** over weed mat so nothing blows onto the concrete.

### Zone D: Right boundary bed (about 1 × 6 m)

- The hedge row stays if it is not lilly pilly (see Section 6). Clip it to a flat top at 1.0–1.2 m so it reads as a wall, not a row of bushes.
- If it is lilly pilly, plan to replace over two years with **Griselinia littoralis 'Broadway Mint'** (kāpuka, native, glossy, tolerates clay) or **Corokia 'Frosted Chocolate'** for a dark hedge against the white house.
- In front of the hedge, one continuous ribbon of **Liriope 'Evergreen Giant'** or **Dianella 'Little Jess'** (0.3 m spacing) for a clean line of contrast.
- Keep the little fruit tree at the far end only if you want the fruit; otherwise remove it. It will never suit the design.

### Zone E: House face and entry

- Plant two large pots (600 mm, black or charcoal) either side of the front door with **Buxus-alternative** spheres: *Corokia* or *Pittosporum* 'Golf Ball'. Skip Buxus; Buxus blight is active in the Waikato.
- Add a strip of *Muehlenbeckia axillaris* groundcover along the house foundation bed so the soil is never bare.

### Zone F: Bins and side gate

- Add a **black batten bin screen** (same profile as the fence, 1.2 m high, 1.5 m long) at right angles to the side fence so bins are hidden from the street but reachable for collection day.

### Zone G: Lighting

- Three low-voltage LED uplights: one on the specimen tree, two washing the hedge in Zone D. One bollard or step light at the letterbox. Warm white (2700–3000 K). Run on a photocell timer. Use a licensed electrician for the transformer connection.

### Zone H: Street verge

- Hamilton City Council owns the berm. Check whether the subdivision plan includes a street tree. If not, ask the council whether you can request one. Do not plant on the berm without approval.

## 6. Checks before you spend money

1. **Identify the existing hedge shrubs.** Take a leaf to a nursery or use the iNaturalist app. If lilly pilly (*Syzygium*), it is a myrtle rust host. It may still be fine, but a top-tier garden should not rely on a plant with a known disease risk. Decide keep or replace.
2. **Covenant.** Read your title's land covenant. Look for: front fence height, bin screening, minimum planting, approved species, letterbox style.
3. **Hamilton City Council District Plan.** A web search summary reported a 1.2 m maximum for fences forward of the front building line in the Special Residential Zone, and 1.8 m for front fences more generally. I could not open the council site from this session. Confirm on hamilton.govt.nz before building the bin screen.
4. **Utility box clearance.** Phone the utility (the label on the box will say). Ask which faces must stay clear and by how much.
5. **Before U Dig.** Request service plans at beforeudig.co.nz before the tree hole or any lighting trenches.
6. **Soil test the hero bed.** Dig a 400 mm hole, fill with water, time the drain. If it takes more than 24 hours, add gypsum and compost and mound the planting 150 mm above grade. Kōwhai and *Astelia* rot in waterlogged clay.
7. **Wind.** Chartwell is not especially windy, but the tree needs staking for its first two years.
